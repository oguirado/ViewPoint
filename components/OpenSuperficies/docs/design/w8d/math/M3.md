### PM-110.2 · stmt-thm-main

Source: [papers/EN-114-000-0007-2-2026-PR-T4-UK-EX-M1-B-AL-M-0239_PM-110.2_Uniform_computation_of_the_squared_logarithmic_k_server_bound.md:3379](/home/user/Documentos/math-router/papers/EN-114-000-0007-2-2026-PR-T4-UK-EX-M1-B-AL-M-0239_PM-110.2_Uniform_computation_of_the_squared_logarithmic_k_server_bound.md:3379); anchor `stmt-thm-main`.

> **Theorem 1**. *There are an absolute constant $C$ and a polynomial $p$ with the following property. Let $n\ge3$, $2\le k<n$, let $d$ be a rational metric on $X=\{1,\ldots,n\}$, and let $s\in X^k$ be an initial labeled tuple, with repetitions allowed. Write $L$ for the total binary encoding length of $n,k,d,s$. A single uniform randomized online algorithm preprocesses these data in at most $p(L)$ bit operations and serves request $r_t$, for every reachable history and internal state, in at most $$p\!\left(L+\left\lceil\log_2(t+1)\right\rceil\right)$$ additional bit operations. It uses only unbiased random bits and moves exactly one chosen server to each request, permitting a zero-length move. For every fixed finite request sequence $\sigma$ independent of those bits, <a id="eq:main-bound"></a>$$\label{eq:main-bound}
>  \mathbb E\mathop{\mathrm{cost}}_{A,s}(\sigma)
>  \le C\bigl(\log(k+1)\bigr)^2\mathop{\mathrm{OPT}}_s(\sigma)+B_{d,k,s},$$ where $B_{d,k,s}$ is finite and independent of $\sigma$ and its length. The algorithm does not need to know that length in advance.*

### PM-110.1 · stmt-thm-main

Source: [papers/EN-114-000-0007-2-2026-PR-T4-UK-EX-M1-B-AL-H-0238_PM-110.1_Squared_logarithmic_randomized_k_server_on_arbitrary_metrics.md:4602](/home/user/Documentos/math-router/papers/EN-114-000-0007-2-2026-PR-T4-UK-EX-M1-B-AL-H-0238_PM-110.1_Squared_logarithmic_randomized_k_server_on_arbitrary_metrics.md:4602); anchor `stmt-thm-main`.

> **Theorem 1**. *There is an absolute constant $C<\infty$ such that, for every integer $k\ge2$, every metric space $(X,d)$ with at least $k+1$ points, and every $s\in X^k$, there exist a randomized online policy $A$ and a finite $B\ge0$ satisfying <a id="eq:main"></a>$$\label{eq:main}
>  \mathbb E\,\mathop{\mathrm{cost}}_{A,s}(\sigma)
>  \le C\bigl(\log(k+1)\bigr)^2\mathop{\mathrm{OPT}}_{X,d,k,s}(\sigma)+B$$ for every finite request sequence $\sigma$. The constant $C$ is independent of every property of the metric. The number $B$ may depend on $(X,d),k,s$, but not on the sequence or its length. The same policy serves all finite sequences and horizons. If the initial positions are distinct, one may take $B=0$.*

## Plain terms

The source claims an expected squared-logarithmic competitive movement-cost bound for randomized k-server policies [CITED]. The uniform-computation version requires a finite rational metric and a request sequence independent of the random bits. This is not a deterministic cache guarantee.

## Limits: when this does not apply

Variable image sizes give a byte-constrained file cache rather than k equal servers. Directed fetch costs, changing metrics, concurrency and adaptive adversaries require separate analysis. Provider prompt-cache retention and hit eligibility cannot be controlled by a local eviction theorem. Neither LRU nor the deterministic proposal inherits the cited competitive ratio.
