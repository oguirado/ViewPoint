# RP4 vision rubric · rp4.e2e-vision.v1

Score the full-frame pixels at the presented view. Use whole-number scores and cite one or more sampled frame indices and their PNG digests for each axis. Describe a visible observation at every cited index. Use successive frames for a motion or timing claim. Where a feature should be visible and is absent, record what is visible in its place. Use the blind response already returned as the record of the initial viewing.

All scores and thresholds below are design targets [TARGET].

| Score | Textures and materials | Silhouette readability | Animation readability | Contact and staging | Technical artefacts |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Required character surface is absent or unreadable. | Required character is absent or fully obscured. | No character movement is visible. | No visible actor–prop interaction or floor relationship. | Missing, black, frozen or unreadable playback prevents review. |
| 2 | Flat proxy or missing textures dominate the visible character. | Character is visible but whole-body pose is illegible. | Camera or graphic movement dominates a static character. | The interaction is implied by cues while bodies never visibly meet. | Severe corruption, geometry failure or discontinuity obscures the event. |
| 3 | Some surface detail is visible, but key materials are hard to distinguish. | Character reads at a glance, but a required limb or prop is lost at the decisive moment. | A pose or brief gesture reads, but the action sequence is unclear. | The set reads, but feet, contact point or resulting change cannot be resolved. | Recurring clipping, deformation, judder or flicker interrupts the action. |
| 4 | Key materials occasionally separate, with major smearing or inconsistent response. | Overall pose separates while multiple decisive areas overlap. | Separate motion beats appear but the transition or direction is confusing. | The active objects are visible, but their relationship stays ambiguous. | Distracting defects persist through a key beat. |
| 5 | Some intentional finishes read, though defects remain visible on key surfaces. | Most body parts read, with a decisive overlap or crop. | The broad action reads, while a required beat or weight shift is ambiguous. | Contact looks plausible, with timing or spatial alignment still unclear. | A discrete defect changes how a key beat reads. |
| 6 | Surfaces largely read, with an obvious local defect during the action. | Limbs and prop mostly read, with interruptions at action speed. | Main beats read with obvious stiffness or uneven transitions. | The interaction reads with visible sliding or an unclear response delay. | Repeated defects distract while the action remains intelligible. |
| 7 | The material contrast holds overall, but a sustained defect competes with it. | The full body reads, with a brief ambiguity at a significant beat. | The sequence is recognizable, but weight transfer or recovery visibly falters. | The causal event reads, but a planted foot or the contact pose visibly slips. | A visible repeated defect draws attention during the action. |
| 8 | Key finishes remain distinguishable during motion with minor local defects. | Body, feet, acting limb and prop remain clear during decisive beats. | The action and its transitions are clear at speed, with minor stiffness. | The floor, contact and subsequent response read in order with minor defects. | Minor artefacts leave contact timing and action readable. |
| 9 | Finishes remain distinct and coherent across the take, with a small blemish. | Pose and prop separate from the background across the take. | Beats, weight transfer and recovery read cleanly with a small blemish. | Stance and contact read clearly and the response timing is unambiguous. | Isolated minor defects have negligible visual impact. |
| 10 | Surface detail is consistently legible and stable under the shot lighting. | Every necessary pose and contact detail remains distinct at the presented scale. | Movement is consistently clear and physically convincing through all beats. | Stance, reach, contact, response and depth remain precise throughout. | No discernible technical artefacts affect the viewed frames or playback. |

The thresholds are at least 8/10 on each axis, at least 34/40 across the first four axes, and at least 43/50 overall [TARGET]. The first four preserve the plan's per-axis and aggregate requirements on a doubled scale. The overall floor preserves its aggregate fraction when the technical axis is included. The orchestrator also matches the blind description to the independently known action and consequence, and verifies technical PASS for the same video.
